# 315 - The outline's disclosure keys and its accessibility (`OG-`, lane 5)

Branch `feat/outline-keys-ax` from master `324686be`. Lane 5 of the hierarchical lists (design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-outline-disclosure-design.md` sections 3, 4, 8; decisions `docs/superpowers/2026-11-15-outline-disclosure-decisions.md`; probe `docs/probes/swiftui-outline.swift` arms `P-K2`..`P-K5`, `P-C1`, `P-A1`, `P-A2`). Class `A` (every behaviour is SwiftUI's measured one); **one new divergence label, 1086** (the ⌥ cascade writes a content list's bindings in one drain after the build, not in the key's turn). `OG-E` and `OG-F` are final for the outline; `OG-M` is new.

**What.** (1) ← → and ⌥ on an outline list's lead row, both forms (the content `List { ForEach { DisclosureGroup(isExpanded:) } }` and `List(_:children:)`/`OutlineGroup`): → expands a collapsed group and does nothing on an expanded group or a leaf; ← collapses an expanded group, selects the parent of a leaf or a collapsed group, and does nothing on a collapsed root; ⌥→ expands a collapsed group and every descendant, ⌥← collapses an expanded group and every expanded descendant; ⌥-click on the chevron cascades the same way. `ControlKeys.outlineDisclosure` is the new row, composed after `listMove`. (2) The accessibility of an outline: outline hints on the list and its rows, expand and collapse actions behind the disabled gate, the disclosed-by row, the cell and triangle of a group row, the new requests `.expand`/`.collapse`, the AppKit `AXDisclosing` set and the disclosure attributes, AccessKit's `TREE`/`TREE_ITEM` with `level`, `expanded`, `EXPAND`/`COLLAPSE` and the nesting under the realised parent row. (3) The nesting carried from lane 4: a content list's row is now the parent of its content's text.

**How.** Keys: `LazyUnitSource` gained `lazyDepth(_:)` and `lazyDisclosure(_:)` (input time, no realisation; `LazyDisclosure` is `isExpanded` read live, `set`, `cascade`); a nested source adds its depth step, a `ForEach` of trees locates the element, `OutlineGroupSource` answers from its index entries and the state table. The key handler reads the lead (`ListSelectionController.leadPosition()`), acts on `table.disclosure(lead)` and, for a parent, `table.depth(_:)` back to the first lower level then `ListSelectionController.select(position:)` (the shared commit of a move, now factored out of `move`, which keeps its behaviour). The cascade is two mechanisms because the two forms store expansion differently (`OG-M`): the data form writes its state slots in the key's turn by walking the data (`OutlineGroupSource.cascade`, iterative); the content form cannot (a binding is only written from input, a group's content needs a layout pass), so the key writes a request into the list's state (`list/$listcascade`), the next build flattens the group, and everything below it, as expanded (`OutlineCascade.direction(for:)`, a static set around the list's layout like `ListRowChrome.isActive`) and each group that was not open enqueues its own write through `LifecycleStore.enqueue`; the window runs them in one drain and builds again inside the same `beginFrame`, so the first presented frame is the final state. The chevron became an `onClick` (a tap gesture's closure reads no modifiers) wrapped in `accessibilityHidden`. Accessibility: `AXNode.outlineHint` (an optional three-byte value, stripped like the other hints; `AXNode` and `Handlers` stay under their pinned sizes), `AccessibilityDisclosure` (an `Action` handler per group row), `Frame.withAccessibilityAdopter` and `AXEmission.adopter` (a row adopts the records made inside it), the builder's `deriveDisclosure` (nearest preceding realised row one level up) and the cell and triangle it synthesises (`OutlineCell`, `OutlineTriangle`, keys no request resolves to an element; a press on the triangle is the row's expand or collapse); `AccessibilityNode` gained `isOutline`, `outlineLevel`, `isExpanded`, `disclosedBy`, `AccessibilityActions` `.expand`/`.collapse`, `AccessibilityRole.cell`, `AccessibilityRequest.expand`/`.collapse`. AppKit overrides `accessibilityDisclosureLevel`, `isAccessibilityDisclosed`, `setAccessibilityDisclosed`, `accessibilityDisclosedByRow`, `accessibilityDisclosedRows` (offered only by an outline row, settable only where an action is advertised). AccessKit: `ACCESSKIT_ROLE_TREE`, `ACCESSKIT_ROLE_TREE_ITEM`, `accesskit_node_set_level` (depth + 1: ARIA's, 1 for a root), `accesskit_node_set_expanded`, `ACCESSKIT_ACTION_EXPAND`/`COLLAPSE` verified against the fetched 0.23 `accesskit.h` (`scripts/fetch-accesskit.py` into a scratch directory); `AccessKitSnapshot.nestingOutlines` nests rows under their realised parent (a row whose parent is not realised hangs under the tree) and drops the cell and triangle.

**Deviations from the brief.** (a) ⌥→ on the content form is NOT written in the key's turn: evaluating a group's content closure off-window at input time runs a `Component`'s body with no `@State` bound and no environment, and a binding is never written from layout (`RW-I`), so the closest is the one-drain mechanism above; measured, the four writes of the five-deep chain have no build between them and the first frame the key presents holds the whole chain, which is divergence 1086. The data form writes in the key's turn. (b) ⌥← on a leaf or a collapsed group acts as ← (the probe measured only ⌥← on an expanded group; the brief's "on a child leaves the parent open" is `K5b`, the expanded child). (c) A content collapse cascade reaches the groups under an expanded ancestor only (a group under an already collapsed child keeps its state); the data form's reaches the same set. (d) The chevron is an `onClick`, not a tap gesture (modifiers). (e) `AccessibilityRole.cell` and the `.expand`/`.collapse` actions are new public cases of `MetalUIPlatform`'s vocabulary (a migration note on `AccessibilityRequest`); `MetalUITesting`'s `performAccessibilityAction` runs the two actions too. (f) Rows of an outline with no `selection:` are now published to a client (they were not).
