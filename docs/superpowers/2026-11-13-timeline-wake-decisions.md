# Timeline wake anchor - decisions

Rulings for the timeline clock's anchor. Prefix `TW-`. Branch `fix/glass-timeline-writes`. **Nothing is published.**
**Next unused: `TW-B`.** No divergence.

## TW-A - the clock is anchored on a frame, never on a deadline

**Report.** Alloy with glass on, typing in a `CodeView`, `METALUI_LATENCY_TRACE_ALL=1`: 191 key frames carried `dirty=state@TimelineSchedule/7` and 158 ticks `state@TimelineSchedule/1`, a frame per display tick while the window was idle, and a key arriving at that dirty window was paced (`why=needsRedraw`, about 15 ms, about 4 frames per key). Without glass 57 such ticks.
**Cause.** `TimelineStore.noteDeadline(atTimestamp:)` (the caret blink's `CV-AC` ask, the hover timers') converted its argument with `date(at:)`, which anchors `clockOffset` (`Date.now - timestamp`, `KF-Y`) at the first non-zero timestamp it is given. When a deadline was the first to read the clock (a focused code view at frame 0, timestamp 0, asks for timestamp 0.5), the clock was anchored on the deadline, up to half a second ahead of the frame: every date was that much early. The wake (a wall-clock `Task`) then fired before the frame's own clock reached the deadline, the frame it drew noted the same deadline, already due, and fired again at once: a frame per tick until the clock caught up, after every toggle again; typing restarts the blink epoch and the spin with it. Whether a run anchors early depends on the phase of the first ask; the simulated clock (`DX-A`) anchors before any frame and could not show it. The retained-refusal hypothesis (a backdrop taints retention) was not the cause: the caret already taints retention with `codeCaretBlink`, and a backdrop on the simulated clock is one frame per key.
**Ruling.** `noteDeadline` takes the noting frame's timestamp (`frameTimestamp:`): the clock is anchored on that, and a deadline is that frame's date plus its distance from the frame; at frame timestamp 0 (no anchor, `KF-Y`) it is the real clock plus the distance. Callers: the caret blink and the hover timers. Pins: `TimelineWakeAnchorTests` (the recorded wake is half a second out from a frame at timestamp 0 and from a key at timestamp 100, red under the old anchor; a backdrop's spaced key is one frame and the blink keeps its schedule, a guard that passes before as well).
