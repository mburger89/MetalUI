## FC-A: a `.focusable()` element takes focus on a primary press

1. **SwiftUI** (`FC1`, `C9`, `C13`, `C15`): a press focuses `.focusable()` (and `.focusable(interactions: .edit)`, `FC3` of the key-focus probe); `Button("x").focusable()` focuses and its tap also fires (`C13`); the inner of two nested focusables, then the outer on its own margin (`C8a`); a `TextField` (`C2`). It does not focus `.focusable(interactions: .activate)` (`FC2`, `C0`), a right press (`C10`), `.disabled(true)` (`C11`), `.allowsHitTesting(false)` written either side of `.focusable()` (`C12`, `C12b`), and a plain `Button`, `Toggle` or `Slider` with Full Keyboard Access off (`C1`, `C3`, `C4`: each arm proves the control itself was hit by logging its own action).
2. **MetalUI**: `Handlers.focusesOnPress` is `isFocusable` and the interactions contain `.edit` or `.automatic`. The three `focusable(_:)` spellings (`StyledElement`, a `KeyboardModifier` layer, and the proposal-path wrapper that delegates to the layer) record **`.automatic` explicitly**, where they recorded `nil`. `nil` stays out: a control sets `isFocusable` directly and records no interactions, so `Button`, `Toggle`, `Slider`, `Stepper`, `Picker` and `ColorPicker` remain Tab stops a click does not focus. No new flag, no new `Handlers` member.
3. **`IX-K` item 2's reason no longer holds.** It refused click focus because a focusable element "registers no pointer hitbox" and making it one "would make every focusable element opaque to the pointer". `KF-F` item 3 built the press region **non-opaque** (`DN-E`'s shape), which blocks no click, hover, wheel, drop or gesture aimed at what it covers; the press still reaches the arena (`C13`: focus and tap). `KF-F`'s own "cost if wrong" named this as a one-line change plus the retirement. A plain `.focusable()` is now exactly `.focusable(interactions: .edit)` for a press.
4. **`focusOnPress`'s ladder is unchanged** (`KF-E` item 5): the innermost click-focusable element under the press on the cover's layer wins (`C8a`), a key region around it resigns only when the focusable is not inside it, a text field focuses itself in the later stage. A focusable press moves focus from a focused field (`RS4`).
5. Controls inside a focusable container: pressing a `Button` inside a `.focusable()` container focuses the container and the button's tap fires (`C16`, added to the plan). A stack's empty space is not a hit target in SwiftUI (`C16b`) unless `.contentShape(Rectangle())` (`C16c`); MetalUI's region is the element's whole bounds, so a press on the empty space of a focusable stack focuses it. That is the same difference as divergence 57's (a layer without a pointer target); it is carried there, not a new row.

## FC-B: divergence 57 gains the covered-focusable readings

`C7` (a focusable covered by a tappable: focus stays where it was, the tappable fires), `C7b` (covered by a plain `Color`: focus stays) and `C7c` (a focusable above a tappable: it focuses, the tap does not fire). MetalUI **agrees on `C7`**: the tappable's opaque hitbox is the cover and the focusable is not its ancestor, so `focusOnPress` does not reach it (`aFocusableCoveredByOrCoveringAnotherElementStillTakesThePress`). It **differs on `C7b` and `C7c`**, both of divergence 57's cause: a plain `Color` is not a pointer target in MetalUI, so the focusable beneath it focuses (`C7b`), and the press region is non-opaque, so a focusable above a tappable focuses and the tap behind it still fires (`C7c`). Row 57 is amended with the two readings and the pin; no new label.

## FC-C: unmeasured

Clicks inside a `ScrollView` and a `List` (the instrument does not reach content there: human check `FC2`), Full Keyboard Access on (divergence 80 keeps it, human check `FC1`), a first click in an inactive window (`FC3`), the ring look on a click-focused `.focusable()` (`FC4`), and the plan's `FK` arms (Tab with FKA off: FKA cannot be switched on from the process). A press on a focusable container's non-hit-testable gap is described in `FC-A` item 5. Also unmeasured: a press on a `.draggable().focusable()` source focuses it (`C17`, measured, press and release without a move) but SwiftUI's drag itself was not read against focus.

## FC-D: behaviour changes for callers

Every `.focusable()` box and every `KeyboardModifier` layer that was `.focusable()` now takes focus on a press, now registers one non-opaque press region (so `Frame.focusOnPressCount` and the hitbox count of a tree of focusables rise by one per focusable), and so a press on it clears a focused `TextField` (`RS4`) and, inside a `.hoverKeyRegion()`, focuses instead of clearing. The opt-out is `.focusable(interactions: .activate)` (FC2). `onKey` on such a box, which heard nothing without focus, now hears keys after a click.

## FC-E: the first drawn frame seats the first `.focusable()`

1. **SwiftUI** (probe `swiftui-initial-focus.swift`): a hosting view focuses its first `.focusable()` view when it first draws, with no input and no key window (`I0`, `I0n`). Skipped: a `Button`, `Toggle`, `Slider`, `Picker` (`LF7`, `I1`, `I6a`..`I6c`), `.activate` (`I7`), `focusable(false)` (`I9`), `.hidden()` (`I11`), `.disabled(true)` (`LF9`). Seated: `.focusable(interactions: .edit)` (`I8`) and a focusable under `.allowsHitTesting(false)` (`I26`). The winner is the topmost by final position, ties in tree order (`G1`..`G4`). An `onAppear` write wins (`I20`); `defaultFocus` has no effect in a hosting view (`I18`, `I21`). A focusable inserted after the first draw is not seated (`L1`); focus cleared from code stays cleared (`L3`).
2. **MetalUI**: `Frame.registerHandlers` notes a **seat candidate** (`FocusRegistry.noteSeatCandidate(id:top:)`) for an element whose handlers are `focusesOnPress` (so exactly the `.automatic` and `.edit` focusables, the same predicate as click focus), behind the disabled and hidden gates and outside every scroller, **not** behind `allowsHitTesting` (`I26`). `top` is the rect's top after the open render effects (`G1`, an `.offset`). The registry keeps only the best (smallest `top`, the first on a tie). `Window.seatInitialFocus` runs right before `reconcileFocusStates()` on the first drawn frame only: if nothing is focused it assigns `focusedElement` (a plain assignment plus one `setNeedsRedraw()`, no `StateTable` write: the next frame's `registerHandlers` writes the `$focus` slot as for any focus) and it flips its flag whether or not a candidate exists (`L1`). It lives in `Window`, so AppKit, SDL and the headless platform agree. Candidates are not journalled and not in the prepaint fingerprint: only the first frame, which replays nothing, reads them.
3. **No harness flag turns seating off.** A test whose premise is "nothing focused" calls `window.focus(nil)` after its first draw (`L3` makes that honest).

## FC-F: what is not a candidate, and what is

1. **A `.focusable(interactions: .edit)` is a candidate** (`I8` measured it seated). It is not a text field.
2. **A launch-visible `TextField` takes no focus** (the user's 2026-11-04 decision; the plan's unlocked `WindowGroup` gate was dropped). Divergence 186 is **narrowed to fields**, not retired: SwiftUI's hosting view focuses a laid-out first field (`LF1`, `LF2`, `LF5b`), MetalUI seats neither. `[TextField, focusable]` and `[focusable, TextField]` seat the focusable.
3. **A focusable inside a `ScrollView` or `List` is not a candidate** (the design's scroller exclusion, `Frame.activeScrollerFrame`). SwiftUI differs for a `ScrollView` (`G5`, `G5o`, `G5h`, `G7`, `G7o`: its content is seated, the plan read "not seated") and agrees for a `List`'s rows (`G6`). A `List` earlier in the tree also holds the first responder itself in SwiftUI, so a focusable after it is not seated (`I13`, `I14`; one before it is, `I13b`); MetalUI seats the later focusable. Both are carried in divergence 186's narrowed text, unpinned beyond the MetalUI side (`aFocusableInsideAScrollViewIsNotSeatedButOneOutsideIs`).
4. **Nesting** (`C8`) is not ruled: the topmost rule picks the outer of a nested pair because its rect starts higher or equal and registers first (`C8a`); the plan's "the outer wins even over an earlier sibling" was not reproduced (`C8b`: the earlier top-aligned sibling won), so no test pins it.

## FC-G: divergence 217, a focused `.focusable()` that is removed

SwiftUI moves focus from a removed focused `.focusable()` to an existing one, never to a newly inserted one, even one inserted above it (`L2`, `L4`, `L5`). MetalUI clears focus (`IX-I`: focus leaves with its identity and does not return). New row 217, pinned by `aFocusedFocusableThatIsRemovedClearsFocusAndNoneTakesItsPlace`. A removed focused `TextField` also leaves no focus (`L6`: the first responder falls to the window; the plan's `nil` `@FocusState` flip was not logged in the probe's run).

## FC-H: what T1 was, and behaviour changes for callers

`swiftui-focus-click.swift`'s trap `T1` ("a lone `.focusable()` takes focus from any click in the window") is the **first-draw seat**: `T1s` reads `a=true` before any event, a Tab first changes nothing (`T1k`), and the same focusable inserted after show takes no focus from a click outside it (`T1g`) or a right press (`T1i`), only from a click on it (`T1h`). PR B's readings stand. For callers: the first `.focusable()` (or `.edit`) of a window is focused after its first draw; `onKey` and `onKeyPress` on it hear keys at once; Tab from that start reaches the **second** stop; a `Keymap` binding predicated on an unfocused chain no longer sees an empty chain; a hover-region key fallback only applies while nothing is focused. `TestWindow` shows the seat after the first draw (`docs/testing.md`).
